# Headache Clinical History Taking and Domain Assessment

## Onset and Timing
- Question intent: Determine the speed of onset and duration of headache symptoms.
- Clinical domain: onset
- Guidance: Distinguish sudden onset (thunderclap reaching peak within seconds to minutes) from gradual onset developing over hours or days.
- Inquire: When did the headache begin, and did it reach maximal intensity immediately or build up gradually?

## Location and Quality
- Question intent: Identify the anatomical location and pain quality of the headache.
- Clinical domain: location, character
- Guidance: Unilateral throbbing or pulsating pain suggests migraine; bilateral band-like pressure suggests tension headache; strictly unilateral periorbital sharp or piercing pain suggests cluster headache.
- Inquire: Is the headache located on one side, both sides, frontal, temporal, or back of the head, and does it feel throbbing, pressing, or sharp?

## Aggravating and Relieving Factors
- Question intent: Evaluate postural changes, physical activities, and environmental triggers.
- Clinical domain: triggers_aggravating, relieving_factors
- Guidance: Pain worsening with coughing, bending forward, or Valsalva suggests raised intracranial pressure; sensitivity to light or sound suggests migraine; relief with rest in a dark room is characteristic of primary headaches.
- Inquire: Does the pain worsen with movement, light, or coughing, and what helps relieve the headache?
